Projectile Motion VR Lab

HSC Physics, Module 5: Advanced Mechanics

On Quest 3: open this page in the Meta Quest Browser and tap Enter VR. The console floats in front of you; point a controller (or pinch with your hand) at a button to press it. If the button says VR isn’t available, open the page in its own browser tab rather than inside another page.

On a computer: drag the scene to look around, scroll to zoom, click the console, and press Space to launch.

Typing exact values: tap any yellow number on the console (launch speed, angle, height or g) to open a number pad. On a computer you can also type the number on your keyboard and press Enter.

Worlds: press the Preset button next to the value of g to choose from all eight planets, the Moon, Phobos, Titan and Pluto. Choosing a world changes the scenery and sets g to that world’s value, using NASA figures (for the gas giants, g is measured at the 1-bar cloud level because they have no solid surface). To try a different g in the same scenery, tap the yellow g value and type it in.

Sound: the cannon booms, the ball whooshes louder and higher as it speeds up, a tick marks each time dot, and there’s a thud on landing. In VR the sounds come from where they happen, so you hear the ball travel. Use the Sound button on the console to mute the effects. Calm background music plays too; the Music button turns it on or off separately.

Pause and read the values: press Pause on the console (or P on a computer) to freeze the projectile mid-flight. While it is paused, a label beside the ball shows the time in flight, its height, the horizontal and vertical velocity, and the resultant velocity with its direction. Press Resume to carry on.

Inspect a point on the path: after the ball lands, press Inspect. Tap anywhere on the time bar, or use the Start, Dot, Fine, Top and End buttons, to move the ball back to any point on the path. Zoom in and out to see that point up close or the whole path, and read the time, position, height, horizontal and vertical velocity, and resultant velocity on the console and on the label beside the ball. On a computer, use the arrow keys (Shift for whole dots) and + / − to zoom. Press Done to return.

School lab: choose School lab from the Preset button to launch a small ball from a lab bench in a classroom, with everything scaled down: launch speeds of 0.5 to 10 m/s, bench heights up to 2 m, and a measuring tape on the floor marked every 10 cm. It is ideal for comparing with a real lab launcher.

Curved ramp (School lab only): press Launcher on the console to switch from the spring launcher to a curved track. Set the release height Δh (how far the ball drops along the track), the ball’s mass and the bench height, then press Launch: the ball rolls down the track and leaves the end of the bench horizontally. Choose Ball slides (u = √(2gΔh), no friction) or Ball rolls (u = √(10gΔh/7), allowing for the energy in the ball’s spin). Changing the mass makes no difference to the motion, because it cancels out of mgΔh = ½mu².

Bench view (School lab only): press View until it says Bench to watch side-on with your eyes exactly level with the launch point. A thin red line marks the launch height, so you can see the path rise above it and fall back through it.

Moving the console in VR: press the Console button at the top of the console to move it Front, Low, Left or Right, or hold the grip button on either controller to pick the console up and put it anywhere. It stays where you leave it as you change views.

Follow view: press View until it says Follow and the camera travels alongside the ball, so you can watch the velocity arrows change up close. In VR this moves you through the air with the ball, which some people find uncomfortable; switch back to Side view if it feels too much.

Try this: turn Answers off, calculate the range and time of flight on paper, then launch to check. Compare 30° and 60° at the same speed, then add launch height and see what changes.
